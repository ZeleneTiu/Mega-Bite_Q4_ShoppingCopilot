Exec decisions:
- Missing price handling (B1): treated as unknown, not disqualifying. No item excluded for lacking price; nulls score neutral rather than zero.
- Workaround: derive category price bands from the ~21% of items with real prices, using percentiles at the deepest category level with enough items, falling back up the category hierarchy when a level is too sparse. An unpriced item is scored on how much its category band overlaps the user's stated budget, discounted by a confidence factor since a band is weaker signal than a real price. Items with real prices keep full weight; band-only items get partial weight; items with neither stay neutral. Bands are internal ranking data only, never shown to the user as a price.
- Bound: price acts as a modifier on the fused RRF score (B3), capped narrowly so it can reorder near-ties but never overturn a strong relevance match. Same approach generalises to other sparse fields (e.g. size, colour) filled by A2.

Findings to hand to the team:
1. CATEGORY SCOPING IS THE ENGINE. Simulator opens with the target's own coarse
   category (last 2 cleaned path segments). Exact-bucket match puts the target in
   the pool 200/200 times with a median pool of 194 items out of 50,000. Worth
   +0.14 technical AND a 114x latency win. That is B4 solved as a side effect.
2. SLOT ERASURE ON OVERRIDE IS HARMFUL HERE (for Person A, task A2). The simulator
   draws its "revoked" old_value from the target product too, so it stays true.
   Erasing it drops intent_override hit rate 0.900 -> 0.333 and mttc 4.47 -> 8.60.
   Recommend: detect the override, reweight toward the new constraint, but do not
   delete the old slot. Spec asks for erasure; measurement says don't.
3. ask_attribute IS THE SINGLE BIGGEST LEVER AND IT SITS WITH A/C, NOT B. In the
   evaluator, attribute "other" bypasses classify_constraint and discloses any two
   undisclosed constraints per turn. Asking "other" vs "feature" is worth +0.228;
   asking nothing at all is worth -0.593. Flagging that this is close to gaming the
   simulator, and there is a human quality score too, so the team should decide.
4. Phrase evidence is currently within noise (+0.004). Kept because it should matter
   more once the private set's hand-authored intent cards paraphrase less literally,
   but it is the first thing to cut if it costs latency.
5. Price hierarchy deferred to the end, as agreed. Measured: budget reaches the
   customer's mouth in 0/200 public sessions, despite 178/200 targets having a real

--- Session 3: B3 fusion + B2 dense half ---

Shipped:
- fusion.py  B3. Reciprocal Rank Fusion. Fuses by rank, not raw score, because
             BM25 (unbounded), cosine (-1..1) and category depth (small int) are
             on incomparable scales and normalising them means constants that need
             retuning whenever any ranker changes. k=60 per Cormack et al.
- engine.py  Rewired to four component rankers fused by RRF: lexical, category
             metadata, phrase evidence, dense. Adds _category_scores, which
             discriminates WITHIN an already-filtered category by how deep in the
             path the customer's words match. --no-fusion keeps the old additive
             scorer reproducible.
- dense.py   B2 semantic half. Two interchangeable backends behind one interface:
             "svd" (TF-IDF + truncated SVD, sklearn only, 46s build, no download)
             and "st" (sentence-transformer, one-line swap). Unit-normalised so
             cosine is a dot product. FAISS IndexFlatIP for unscoped queries only;
             pool-restricted queries use a numpy gather, which is faster once the
             category scope has cut the pool to ~200 rows.

Exec decision (B2/B3): DENSE SHIPS ATTACHED BUT WEIGHTED 0.0.
  Monotonic: every gram of dense weight costs score, and any non-zero weight costs
  ~40x eval latency (2.0s -> 70s). Cause is structural, not a bad encoder: the
  simulator quotes the target product's own features/details verbatim, so there is
  almost no paraphrase gap for semantics to close, and dense similarity just blurs
  a ranking that exact matching already got right. Engine skips the dense
  computation entirely at weight 0 rather than computing then discarding.
  Kept in the codebase because B2 requires the dual index, it is built and tested,
  and it is one config line away if the private set paraphrases instead of quotes.
  Qualitatively it does work: "something sparkly for a wedding" returns bridal
  rhinestone heels. Good demo material for D5 even though the benchmark ignores it.

Caveat on the dense claim: measured with the SVD/LSA backend only. The
sentence-transformer backend is written but UNMEASURED, because huggingface.co was
blocked in the dev sandbox. Expect the same conclusion since the cause is
structural, but say "untested" not "no better" when presenting it.

Caveat on the weight sweep: n=200, so the standard error on hit rate is about
0.012. Differences under ~0.02 (e.g. phrase 1.0 vs 2.0, 0.8732 vs 0.8740) are
noise, and were NOT treated as real. The large steps (0.107 -> 0.796 -> 0.857) are
far outside it. Chose round mid-plateau weights over sweep maxima to limit
overfitting to the public 200.

--- Session 4: P1 DONE. Phrasing robustness. ---

Steps taken:

1. Built ANNA_dump/perturb_eval.py before changing any code, to get an honest
   "before" number. It swaps the simulator's sentence templates for paraphrases
   while preserving identical information and identical disclosure logic, so any
   score change is attributable to parsing and nothing else. Three styles:
     clean    the organiser's own templates (control)
     natural  conversational rewording, different markers
     terse    markers stripped entirely, constraints stated bare

2. Measured the old parser. Result was worse than feared:
     clean    0.8732
     natural  0.0000
     terse    0.0000
   Verified this was real and not a broken harness by printing the generated
   messages and the parser output side by side. Messages were well-formed and
   information-equivalent; the parser returned category=None, constraints=[] for
   every single one. The engine was 100% dependent on the organiser's exact
   wording. The public 0.8732 was, at that point, not a claim about retrieval
   quality at all.

3. Rewrote category detection as RECOGNITION rather than EXTRACTION. Instead of
   pulling a span out of "looking for X", find_label matches the message against
   the closed set of 1,115 category labels the catalog can actually produce:
   template fast path, then longest verbatim label anywhere in the message, then
   weighted token overlap for near misses (necklace/necklaces). Matching against
   a known finite set is both more robust and more precise than guessing which
   span of an arbitrary sentence is the category.

4. Rewrote constraint extraction to not need marker phrases. Takes the whole turn
   and removes what is known NOT to be a constraint: the category already stated,
   and any leading clause that announces rather than states. Keeps two forms.
   The stripped form feeds the word-matching query; every form feeds phrase
   evidence, where a candidate that occurs in no document simply scores nothing.

5. Three regressions found and fixed along the way, each caught by measuring
   rather than by reading the code:
   a. Later turns re-detected a category. "The things that matter to me are
      leather" was read as the leather jackets category. Fix: category is sticky,
      established once from the opening turn.
   b. Splitting segments on the word "and" shredded real product phrases
      ("Drop and Dangle"). 19 of 80 sampled constraints contain " and ". Fix:
      "and" now only produces EXTRA candidates alongside the whole segment.
   c. THE BIG ONE. Allowing an override turn to re-detect the category dropped
      intent_override from 0.967 to 0.833 on its own, and accounted for the
      entire clean-style regression; browsing, buying and boundary never moved.
      Cause: an override turn names a new constraint and no category, so the
      fuzzy matcher always returned something, silently moving the scope off the
      target. Fix: an established category can only be replaced by one named
      verbatim, never by the fuzzy matcher.

    rank 4-10   41  (20.5%)
    missed       3
  Fixing only the 2-3 band would take MRR to 0.8135. Also noticed: sessions that
  first hit on turn 1 had median rank 2, sessions hitting on turn 2+ had median
  rank 1. More evidence means a sharper answer, so answering early is often
  answering worse.

THE LEVER: hold back a low-evidence first answer.
  The metric maths rewards it. Answering a vague turn 1 and landing the target at
  rank 2 ends the session at reciprocal rank 0.50. Asking once more and landing
  rank 1 scores 1.00. The extra turn costs efficiency, but efficiency is weighted
  0.2 against MRR's 0.3, so the trade is favourable. Confirmed empirically, not
  assumed.

  Evidence is counted in DISTINCT INFORMATIVE WORDS, not constraint segments.
  Segment counts move with phrasing ("leather and 100% Leather" is one segment
  conversationally, two tersely) and P1 exists precisely so behaviour does not
  depend on wording. Word counts are stable across all three tested styles.
  Segment-based gating also peaked lower (0.9021) and cost a hit; word-based
  peaked higher and cost none.

  Tuning, with a proper peak and degradation on both sides (not an edge effect):
    words>=4  cap 3   technical 0.9092  <- chosen
    words>=6  cap 3   technical 0.9060
    words>=8  cap 3   technical 0.8992
    words>=14 cap 4   technical 0.8810
  Past the peak MRR plateaus around 0.86 but MTTC blows out and efficiency eats
  the gain. cap=3 is a safety net: hit rate is weighted 0.5, so the agent must
  never keep stalling for a better rank. Past turn 3 it answers with what it has.
